A rock, a wall or a sandbag set on a terrain meets it in a hard line: grey stone on brown mud, as if it had been put down a moment ago. Give the model's material a **Ground blend**, and its foot takes on the ground's own look, the earth or snow it stands in creeping up it, so it sits in the ground.

## Blending a model

Select the model's material in the Project panel and set its **Ground blend**: how many metres up from where the model meets the terrain it takes the ground's look. About 0.3 suits a rock or a sandbag, 0.6 a boulder half buried, 1 or more a ruin sunk in drifts.

Where the model meets the ground it is all ground, the ground's surfaces as the terrain draws them there, facing as the ground faces, so no seam shows. Higher up it eases back to its own material, the edge wandering rather than level. The ground creeps further up what faces up, such as a ledge, than up a sheer side.

The ground's look follows the terrain: paint mud round a rock, and its foot turns muddy too. Over a hole in the terrain, or off every terrain, the model is drawn as its material alone.

## What it costs

Each pixel of a blended model looks up the terrain under it, and near the ground samples the ground's surfaces as the terrain does, so give a ground blend only to what stands in the ground. A material without one costs nothing more.
