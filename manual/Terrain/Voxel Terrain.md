A **Voxel Terrain** is ground you can dig into and build up anywhere, with caves, overhangs and arches, its surface running smoothly rather than in steps. It is a [[Block Worlds|Block World]] drawn smooth: the same kinds of block, the same generated ground and the same brushes and scripts, but each block also keeps how full it is where the ground's surface passes, and the surface runs between the blocks' middles as full as each one is.

## Making a Voxel Terrain

Select an Obj and pick **Add Component ▸ Terrain ▸ Voxel Terrain**. It starts as flat ground 64 blocks a side and 8 up, on a new asset of its own in the project's **Voxels** folder, named for its Obj: `Voxels/Ground.blockworld`. Its section in the Inspector reads **Voxel Terrain**.

Every setting of a Block World works the same here (see [[Block Worlds]]). Under **Settings**, **Smooth** is what makes it a Voxel Terrain: untick it and the same blocks are drawn as cubes, tick it on any Block World and its blocks are drawn smooth.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Block size | How many metres a block is a side: the finest detail its surface has |
| Smooth | Whether its surface runs smoothly between its blocks, or its blocks are cubes |
| Ground | **Flat**, **Generated** by a world generator, or a **Planet** (see below) |

## Generated ground

Generated by a [[Generating Terrain|world generator]], a Voxel Terrain lies on the generator's heights to within a fraction of a block, not in steps a block high: every block near the surface keeps how far the surface is above or below its middle. **Overhangs** push the surface in and out so cliffs lean, and **Caves** hollow the ground below, their walls as smooth as the surface. It can go on [[Block Worlds#Without end|without end]] as a Block World can, its far rings drawn smooth too.

## Planets

With **Ground** set to **Planet**, its ground lies all round a middle at its Obj's position, a world of its own to walk round:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Generator | The `.worldgen` its hills, mountains, coasts and climate come from |
| Radius | How far its sea is from its middle, in metres |
| Overhangs, Caves | As for generated ground |

Its ground is as high over its sea as its generator's heights are over the generator's sea level, read all round it in three dimensions, so it has no seams and its poles are no different from anywhere else, but colder: up to 30 °C colder than round its middle, so snow covers them where a kind of block covers by temperature. Without a generator it is a ball, level with its sea. Its caves reach 64 m under its surface; below them it is its base block. Gullies a generator wears into flat ground are left out on a planet.

A planet a few hundred metres round shows only a small piece of its generator's world, often a single kind of ground. Make the generator's **Scale** smaller to fit its coasts and ranges round it.

Physics pulls everything with a Rigidbody near a planet towards its middle, as strongly as gravity pulls elsewhere and in place of it: near is within four times its radius of its middle. A ball dropped beside it falls onto its side and comes to rest there.

A planet is made all at once, never without end, so a large one takes a while: one 200 m across takes a few seconds to make and draw, most of it for its caves. Not done yet: characters keep their own up, so a Character Controller walks only on its top; and far from the scene's middle, past about 10 km, things are drawn and moved less exactly, as everywhere, until a floating origin comes.

## How it is drawn

Each kind of block is drawn with its **Top** where the ground faces up, its **Bottom** where it faces down, and its **Material** where it is steep, blended as the ground turns: wholly its top on slopes up to about 25 degrees, wholly its sides past about 50. A kind of grass with a grass top and a rock material covers gentle hills in grass and turns to rock on the cliffs, with no painting.

The surface takes the way the ground faces from the blocks round it, so light runs smoothly over hills. It casts the sun's shadows, the GI's rays see it, and physics stands things on it and rays meet it where it is drawn. Chunks meet without gaps.

## Building

The brushes under **Build** work as balls whose surface lies between blocks:

| Brush | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Place | Adds ground of the picked kind, a ball **Size** blocks across, against the face under the pointer |
| Break | Takes the ground in the ball away |
| Paint | Turns the ground in the ball to the picked kind, as full as it was |

Drag to go on, Shift to break while placing. Each stroke is one step to undo. A **Model** brush builds its model block by block, each block wholly ground.

## Voxel Terrain from scripts

**Blocks** in a script works on a Voxel Terrain as on any Block World. `Blocks.Ball` and `Blocks.Break` with a radius make round shapes, their surface between blocks; one block set by `Blocks.Set` or `Blocks.Place` is wholly ground or wholly air.

```csharp
// A shell's crater, 2.5 m across.
Blocks.Break(impact, 1.25f);
```

What you build and dig is kept beside the asset as for any Block World, with how full each changed block is, and a save of the scene keeps what scripts changed.
