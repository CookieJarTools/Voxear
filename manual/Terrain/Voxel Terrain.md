A **Voxel Terrain** is ground you can dig into and build up anywhere, with caves, overhangs and arches, its surface running smoothly rather than in steps. It is a [[Block Worlds|Block World]] drawn smooth: the same [[Blocks|blocks]], the same generated ground and the same brushes and scripts, but each block also keeps how full it is where the ground's surface passes, and the surface runs between the blocks' middles as full as each one is.

## Making a Voxel Terrain

Select an Obj and pick **Add Component ▸ Terrain ▸ Voxel Terrain**. It starts as flat ground 64 blocks a side and 8 up, on a new asset of its own in the project's **Voxels** folder, named for its Obj: `Voxels/Ground.blockworld`. Its section in the Inspector reads **Voxel Terrain**.

Every setting of a Block World works the same here (see [[Block Worlds]]). Under **Settings**, **Smooth** is what makes it a Voxel Terrain: untick it and the same blocks are drawn as cubes, tick it on any Block World and its blocks are drawn smooth.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Block size | How many metres a block is a side, from 0.1 to 10: the finest detail its surface has |
| Smooth | Whether its surface runs smoothly between its blocks, or its blocks are cubes |
| Ground | **Flat**, or **Generated** by a world generator (see below) |

## Far away

Where its surface is flat or gently curved, its triangles are merged into larger ones, the surface moving no more than a tenth of a block, so a flat field is a few triangles rather than two a block. Each chunk is kept twice more, merged further, for drawing far away: the further a chunk is from the camera, the coarser it is drawn, by the same rule that picks a model's level of detail. Chunks meet without gaps whichever way each is drawn, and what you stand on is always the finest.

## Generated ground

Generated by a [[Generating Terrain|world generator]], a Voxel Terrain lies on the generator's heights to within a fraction of a block, not in steps a block high: every block near the surface keeps how far the surface is above or below its middle. **Overhangs** push steep ground in and out so cliffs lean and bulge, and **Caves** hollow the ground below, their walls as smooth as the surface. Both can move into the generator (see [[Block Worlds#Overhangs and caves in the generator|Overhangs and caves in the generator]]), where a **Cave Network** adds [[Caves|caves of rooms]] you make as prefabs, their walls smooth too. It can go on [[Block Worlds#Without end|without end]] as a Block World can, its far rings drawn smooth too, each ring's ground running on across it with no grooves, and meeting the finer ring inside it behind a short skirt.

Where the ground is pushed, each block keeps its true distance from the surface, so a leaning cliff is as smooth as a field. The top block follows slopes as a skin, about a block thick whichever way the ground faces, with the under block below it. Walls steeper than about 70° and the undersides of overhangs are bare: they show the base block. A terrain with Overhangs at 0 on a generator with no caves or overhangs of its own keeps its layers a column at a time, as it always did.

## How it is drawn

Each block is drawn with its **Top** where the ground faces up, its **Bottom** where it faces down, and its **Material** where it is steep, blended as the ground turns: wholly its top on slopes up to about 25 degrees, wholly its sides past about 50. A grass block with a grass top and a rock material covers gentle hills in grass and turns to rock on the cliffs, with no painting.

Where two kinds of ground meet, they blend across the triangles between them, so sand runs into grass over a soft edge. Each point of the surface takes the kind of the ground nearest the surface round it, so a thin top layer of grass stays grass on a slope and the dirt under it does not show through.

The surface takes the way the ground faces from the blocks round it, so light runs smoothly over hills. It casts the sun's shadows, the GI's rays see it, and physics stands things on it and rays meet it where it is drawn. Chunks meet without gaps.

Water is smooth in a Voxel Terrain too, its surface meeting the ground at the shore: a generated sea lies flat at its sea level. It flows as it does in a Block World (see [[Block Worlds#Water|Water]]).

## Building

The brushes under **Build** work as balls whose surface lies between blocks:

| Brush | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Place | Adds ground of the picked block, a ball **Size** blocks across, against the face under the pointer |
| Break | Takes the ground in the ball away |
| Paint | Turns the ground in the ball to the picked block, as full as it was |

Drag to go on, Shift to break while placing. Each stroke is one step to undo. A **Model** brush builds its model block by block, each block wholly ground.

## Voxel Terrain from scripts

**Blocks** in a script works on a Voxel Terrain as on any Block World. `Blocks.Ball` and `Blocks.Break` with a radius make round shapes, their surface between blocks; one block set by `Blocks.Set` or `Blocks.Place` is wholly ground or wholly air.

```csharp
// A shell's crater, 2.5 m across.
Blocks.Break(impact, 1.25f);
```

What you build and dig is kept beside the asset as for any Block World, with how full each changed block is, and a save of the scene keeps what scripts changed.
